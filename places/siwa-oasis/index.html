Siwa Oasis

Egypt — Western Desert, near the Libyan border

The oasis where Alexander the Great was told he was a god.

A small oasis ringed by dunes and mountains, home to a community of over 20,000 people and to the ruins of the Temple of Amun. In 331 BC Alexander the Great came here to consult the oracle before his Egyptian campaign; the priests told him he was the son of Zeus, and he spent the rest of his life banking on it. The ancient temples, some with Ptolemaic and Roman reliefs, are still standing in the shade of date palms.

Field note

Siwa’s dates are famous in Egypt; the oasis also produces a natural, highly concentrated salt that was traded across the Sahara for centuries.
