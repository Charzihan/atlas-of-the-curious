Aletsch Glacier

Aletschgletscher

Switzerland — Valais, the Swiss Alps

The longest and thickest glacier in the Alps — a blue river of ice.

The Aletsch Glacier drains the highest peaks of the Swiss Alps — the Matterhorn, the Mönch, and the Dufourspitze — into a single, 23-kilometre tongue of ice, the largest glacier in Europe outside the polar regions. From the high viewing points and the cable-car stations above it, the glacier reads as a slow river of blue ice, its surface crevassed and banded with layers of debris that mark each winter's snowfall. Like most Alpine glaciers it has been retreating for over a century, but it is still the dominant ice body in the range.

Field note

The Aletsch is up to 900 metres thick at its centre and has been measured since 1893; it feeds the Rhône River and is one of the classic high-Alpine trekking routes.
