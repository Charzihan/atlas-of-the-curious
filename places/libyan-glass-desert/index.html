The Libyan Glass Desert

صحراء الزجاج الليبي

Libya — Western Sahara, near the Egyptian border

Dunes of natural glass, fused by a 29,000-year-old impact.

About 29,000 years ago a meteorite struck this stretch of sand with enough energy to flash-melt the silica into glass. Wind and sand have since ground the glass into millions of translucent shards, so the dunes sparkle like crushed diamonds and will slice anything that crosses them barefoot. It is one of the few places on Earth where you can walk on a field of natural meteorite glass.

Field note

The glass, called 'Libyan desert glass', was already being used for flint-like cutting tools 30,000 years ago — among the earliest human use of meteorite material.

Best time to visitOct–Mar, when the desert is cool enough to cross
