Kakadu National Park

Australia — Northern Territory, Australia

A landscape where the rock art, the wetlands, and the billabongs have coexisted for 65,000 years.

Kakadu is one of the wettest and oldest landscapes on Earth, a mix of savanna, wetlands, and escarpments where waterfalls and billabongs flood and dry with the seasons. Its rock shelters hold thousands of painted and stencilled images made by the Bininj/Mungguy people over tens of thousands of years — among the longest continuous records of human culture anywhere. In the wet season the park is a vast, flooded bird haven; in the dry it is a network of clear waterholes and sandstone escarpments.

Field note

Kakadu's rock art includes the distinctive 'X-ray' painting style and some of the oldest known depictions of now-extinct animals; the park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Best time to visitMay–October (the dry season), when tracks are passable and the waterfalls still run
