Milford Sound

Piopiotahi

New Zealand — Fiordland, South Island

A drowned valley where the mountains fall straight into the sea.

Milford Sound is one of the 'fiords' of southwestern New Zealand: a deep, narrow inlet carved by glaciers and then flooded as sea levels rose after the last ice age. The result is a body of water ringed by 1,200-metre cliffs that sheer straight into a dark green sea, with waterfalls plunging from the ridgelines and seals hauling out on the black beaches. On a still, rainy morning the mist hanging in the fiord gives it an almost prehistoric, green-and-black atmosphere.

Field note

Milford Sound is the single most-visited natural site in New Zealand; the fiord is over 5 km long, and a heavy rainstorm can add several metres of depth as the rivers feed it.
