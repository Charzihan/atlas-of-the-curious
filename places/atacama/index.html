The Atacama Desert

Chile — Antofagasta / Calama, northern Chile

The driest non-polar place on Earth — and a window onto the sky.

The Atacama is the world's driest desert: some weather stations have never recorded rain, and the salt flats and hyper-arid valleys hold some of the oldest, most stable soils on Earth. Because the air is so dry and the region so dark and high, it is also one of the finest places on the planet to look up: the great professional observatories sit on its high plateaus, and the clear nights make it a natural dark-sky sanctuary.

Field note

Parts of the Atacama are so dry that NASA has used them to test and build Mars-landing hardware, because the chemistry and climate best resemble the Martian surface.

Best time to visitApril–November (the dry season); clear nights year-round for stargazing
