Sigiriya Rock Fortress

සීගිරිය

Sri Lanka — North Central Province, Sri Lanka

A palace built on a 200-metre column of rock, half ruin, half legend.

In the 5th century the king Kassapa built Sigiriya as a fortress-and-palace on top of a sheer 180-metre column of rock, and today it is one of the best-preserved monuments of the ancient world. The climb up is itself a set piece: a series of moats and gardens, the famous 'lion gate' where the visitor grips a pair of massive carved paws to pass beneath a crouched lion, and the painted frescoes of 'the fair maidens' along the cliff face. The summit is a level terrace where the palace once stood, now open to the sky.

Field note

The frescoes of Sigiriya, dating from around the 5th century, are among the earliest and best-preserved examples of secular courtly painting in the Indian Ocean world; the site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
