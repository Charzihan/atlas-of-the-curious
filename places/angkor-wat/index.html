Angkor Wat

អង្គរវត្ត

Cambodia — Siem Reap, northwestern Cambodia

The largest religious monument on Earth, carved as a model of the Hindu cosmos.

Built in the 12th century under King Suryavarman II, Angkor Wat is a model of the Hindu universe: five central towers represent the peaks of Mount Meru, a moat represents the cosmic ocean, and the bas-relief gallery inside holds 600 metres of carved narrative, including the most famous scene in Khmer art, the Churning of the Ocean of Milk. The temple was built to face west — unlike earlier Khmer temples — which has led some scholars to suggest it was also a funerary temple for the king himself.

Field note

Angkor Wat has never been fully looted; the reason is debated, but one theory is that the temple’s westward orientation made it too large and complex for the Khmer looting parties to ransack in a single season.
