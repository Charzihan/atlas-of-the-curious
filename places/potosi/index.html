Urban Oddities

Potosí

Bolivia — Cerro Rico, southern Bolivia

A city built on top of a mountain of silver — and the mountain is still being mined.

Potosí grew so fast in the 16th century, after Spanish colonists found Cerro Rico (the 'Rich Hill'), that it became the largest city in the Americas. The silver mined here paid for the Spanish Empire’s wars for over a century. The mountain is now nearly hollow, its surface riddled with thousands of mine entrances, and it is still being worked today. The city sits at 4,090 metres, the highest major city in the world, and the air is so thin that visitors often feel the altitude within an hour.

Field note

Cerro Rico is so hollowed out that it is slowly collapsing; engineers have estimated it has 100–200 years of structural stability left at current mining rates.
