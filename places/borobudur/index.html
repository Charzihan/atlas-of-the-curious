Borobudur

Indonesia — Central Java, Indonesia

The world's largest Buddhist monument, built as a three-dimensional map of the cosmos.

Borobudur was raised in the 9th century as a stepped monument in the shape of a Buddhist cosmology: a lower base of stacked reliefs depicting the world of desire, a middle ring of square terraces covered in over 2,600 carved panels, and an upper dome of round terraces with 72 small stupas, each holding a Buddha. Climbing it is a slow journey from the earthward lower levels to the serene, open top, with the volcanic hills of Java spread out below. For many centuries it was buried under ash and jungle until it was rediscovered in the 19th century.

Field note

Borobudur is the largest Buddhist monument in the world; its reliefs include around 1,460 panels and 504 Buddha statues, and it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
