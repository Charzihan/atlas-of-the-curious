Rapa Nui (Easter Island)

Rapa Nui

Chile — Polynesia, eastern Pacific Ocean

A volcanic island in the middle of the Pacific, carved with 900 stone heads.

Rapa Nui, about 3,500 km from South America and 2,000 km from the nearest inhabited island, is covered with moai: nearly 1,000 monolithic stone figures, most carved between 1250 and 1500 AD. The largest, on Rano Raraku volcano, is over 20 metres tall and weighs around 70 tonnes. The moai were carved from a single source of volcanic tuff, and the Rapa Nui people had no metal tools, no wheeled vehicles, and no draft animals — they moved them, somehow, up to 15 kilometres across the island.

Field note

The 2017 study by Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo suggested the moai were 'walked' upright using ropes in a rocking motion, rather than dragged on logs — a method that would explain the lack of tree stumps and soil disturbance at the moai sites.
