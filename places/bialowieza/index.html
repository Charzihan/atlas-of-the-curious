Białowieża Forest

Poland / Belarus — southeastern Poland and northeastern Belarus

One of the last primeval lowland forests left in all of Europe.

Białowieża is a huge block of forest that has been left largely untouched since the 19th century, and is the only place in Europe where the Eurasian bison still roam wild. The forest is also one of the few last pieces of primeval temperate forest on the continent: ancient oaks and lindens, some over 500 years old, stand in a web of moss, fungi, and deadwood that has been undisturbed for thousands of years. It was among the first sites in the world to be placed under international protection.

Field note

The forest's primeval stands are a UNESCO World Heritage Site; it is home to the last wild European bison and to one of the largest colonies of white storks in Europe.

Best time to visitMay–June and September, when the light is warm and the animals are most active
