Istanbul

Türkiye — spanning Europe and Asia, at the Bosphorus

The only city that is, literally, in two continents at once.

Istanbul straddles the Bosphorus, the narrow strait that separates Europe from Asia, which makes it the only major city on two continents. It was, in turn, Byzantium, Constantinople, and the capital of the Ottoman Empire, and it stacks those layers in a single walk: Roman walls and the Hagia Sophia from the 6th century, Ottoman mosques with cascading domes, and a working bazaar where merchants have traded since the 15th century. The Bosphorus ferries that carry passengers between the two continents run every few minutes.

Field note

Istanbul's Grand Bazaar, with over 4,000 shops, and the nearby Spice Bazaar are among the oldest and largest covered markets in the world; the city was the capital of three successive empires.
